Most AI tools are reactive. You open them, ask something, get an answer, close them. They're useful, but they're not part of your operation — they're a resource you visit.

NanoClaw is different. It's a dedicated AI agent that runs continuously inside a specific business environment, configured around a specific workflow, operating on company data rather than generic training data. You don't visit it. It's running.

Here's what that means in practice.

## What a Dedicated AI Agent Is

A dedicated AI agent is a system built to handle a defined class of tasks autonomously — receiving inputs, applying logic, calling tools, and producing outputs — without requiring a human to initiate each run.

The important word is *dedicated*. A general-purpose AI assistant can do a lot of things adequately. A dedicated agent does one class of things well, reliably, and at volume — because it's been configured specifically for that workflow, with that organization's data, context, and rules.

The difference is meaningful. A general assistant answering questions about your business will guess at context it doesn't have. A dedicated agent built on your internal data and configured around your actual processes doesn't guess — it knows.

## What NanoClaw Specifically Does

NanoClaw is the agent platform underlying the NanoClaw for Business offering. It is built to handle multi-step workflows that involve:

- Receiving inputs in variable formats (emails, documents, messages, API events)
- Applying organization-specific rules and logic
- Calling external tools and systems (CRMs, databases, ticketing systems, communication platforms)
- Producing structured outputs, taking actions, or routing to humans when the decision exceeds its configured scope

It maintains persistent state — so it remembers what it's done, what's in progress, and what it's waiting on. It logs its reasoning so the work is auditable. And it operates within defined guardrails, so it never acts beyond what it's been authorized to do.

## What Business Problems It Fits

The right NanoClaw use case is a workflow that is:

- **High volume** — running many times a day, not a few times a month
- **Rule-based but with variable inputs** — consistent logic applied to inconsistent formats
- **Consequential enough to matter, routine enough to not require judgment** — the kind of work that's important but currently absorbs disproportionate human time

A few concrete scenarios:

**Inquiry triage and routing.** Incoming customer or partner communications need to be read, categorized, and routed to the right person or system. The logic is consistent; the inputs vary. A dedicated agent handles this at volume, around the clock, without a queue building up.

**Document review and extraction.** Contracts, applications, reports, and submissions need to be reviewed for specific information and flagged if something is missing or out of range. An agent can process these as they arrive, extract the relevant fields, and surface only the ones that need human attention.

**Operational monitoring and response.** Some business processes generate signals that need to be noticed and acted on — inventory levels, service conditions, scheduling conflicts. An agent watching for those signals can respond faster and more consistently than a person checking a dashboard.
